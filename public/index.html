COMPLETE RESEARCH TASK WALKTHROUGH

From a trading question to a reviewed decision

This is a step-by-step account of the completed Tesla investigation, assembled from its preserved findings and evidence record. It is a historical reconstruction, not a contemporaneous prediction or a recreation of missing verbatim agent messages.

01

The question and research boundary

Question examined: How much can Tesla's known Q2 delivery performance and published earnings expectations tell us about its earnings outlook before results are released?

This is a plain-language description of the research task, not a claim about the exact wording of the original user input.

02

Evidence available before the cutoff

The agents received a dated packet with five identified source records. These are the original documents, not post-release commentary.

  1. Tesla Q1 2026 Update22 Apr · prior-quarter revenue and operating context
  2. Tesla Q1 SEC Form 10-Q22 Apr · regulatory earnings record

Source pages display different timestamp precision. Dates are preserved as published; later earnings results were not part of this pre-event packet.

03

ATLAS builds a mixed thesis

ATLAS identified a reported delivery beat but also uncertainty about what it implied for earnings. It did not establish a reliable price forecast.

C1 · Delivery performance

Reported Q2 deliveries of 480,126 against Tesla's company-compiled average estimate of 406,024.

C2 · Market-price inference

Considered the 374.55 USDT pre-cutoff token close when discussing market expectations. A single candle does not establish valuation or investor sentiment.

C3 · Financial expectations

Considered the published Q2 revenue and EPS consensus alongside Q1 revenue. Estimates do not establish realized Q2 performance.

04

VEX challenges the interpretation

VEX questioned whether the company-compiled estimates could stand for the whole market's expectations, whether one Bitget candle supported a broader sentiment claim, and whether comparing estimates against the previous quarter was sufficient to infer growth.

Some VEX alternatives, including speculation about channel stuffing and token mechanics, had no support in the packet. VEX and SEAL also incorrectly suggested Tesla's compiled sell-side consensus was not sell-side. Those statements are model errors, not facts established by the sources.

05

SEAL audits the claims

C1CONTESTED

The delivery figures are source-backed, but the broader inference remains disputed.

C2UNRESOLVED

The price observation alone does not establish market-wide sentiment or valuation.

C3CONTESTED

The estimate comparison is not enough for a confident financial conclusion.

Recorded assessment: The packet was insufficient for a confident judgment about Tesla's financial performance or overall market sentiment. These dispositions are the model's actual recorded classifications, not independent proof that each rationale was correct.

THE TRADING DESK TAKEAWAY

Strong deliveries are not the same thing as a complete earnings thesis.

A trader reading this case should not treat the delivery beat or a single token candle as sufficient evidence of earnings quality or market sentiment. The research leaves open questions about realized financial results, expectations and how the token price behaves across a broader window. VEYRIN surfaces those gaps so the human can decide what to investigate next.

This is an actionable research checklist, not a buy/sell instruction or a claimed profitable signal. The five dated source links are listed above, and the public research method explains the signing and verification boundaries.

How VEYRIN works

Trace which evidence can enter a case, how each role uses it, and what verification establishes.

Start with dated evidence

Sources retain their published date and timestamp precision. A date-only source is never given an invented clock time. Unknown source IDs and evidence after the cutoff are rejected.

Bitget comparisons use completed 15-minute candles from the selected Reality stock token, quoted in USDT. Instrument identity and time windows are checked before use.

Three roles, different jobs

ATLAS builds the case. VEX challenges specific claims. SEAL audits their evidence support. They are separate passes through one configured model—not three independent providers. Each stage has a distinct job, and server-side validators reject unsupported citations and malformed outputs.

Seal the research before reveal

The server calculates a SHA-256 digest of the canonical research record and signs its payload. Verification checks the bytes and signature before an explicit reveal can access post-event observations. Save the receipt yourself: there is no permanent shared-run database.

What this does not prove

  • Historical reconstruction cannot prove a model did not learn the outcome during training.
  • A verified receipt demonstrates integrity since sealing, not the date the research was written, model foresight, or market causality.
  • Two selected candles are not a continuous chart or a claimed investment return.
  • Signatures depend on the configured signing key. Changing it invalidates older signatures.
  • Download your receipt to retain it. Browser sessions do not have permanent shared record links.
  • This implementation supports the curated NVIDIA and Tesla cases. It places no orders, handles no user funds, and offers no investment advice.
